A work addressed to a reader trained in philosophy and troubled by apparent conflicts with Jewish scripture. Maimonides interprets difficult biblical terms, discusses divine attributes, creation, prophecy and providence, and argues for a negative theology.

## Why it matters

It is a central work of medieval Jewish philosophy and shaped Christian scholastics such as Aquinas.
