Diamond asks why some Eurasian societies came to hold technological and political power over much of the world. He argues that differences in geography, domesticable plants and animals, and the diseases that followed from them, rather than differences in human ability, explain these outcomes.

## Why it matters

It is one of the most widely read attempts to explain world history in terms of environment, and it set the terms for many later debates on long-run development.
