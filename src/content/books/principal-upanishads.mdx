A group of philosophical Sanskrit texts, usually counted as about a dozen principal works (including the Brihadaranyaka, Chandogya, Isha, Kena, Katha and Mandukya), forming the final layer of the Vedic corpus. They discuss the nature of the self (atman), ultimate reality (brahman), death, and liberation.

## Why it matters

They underpin the Vedanta schools of Indian philosophy and have influenced later Hindu thought, as well as Western readers after the Latin translation of 1801–02. Commentators including Shankara and Ramanuja developed contrasting interpretations.
