A long narrative poem in three parts (Inferno, Purgatorio, Paradiso), with 100 cantos in all (the Inferno has 34, including an introductory canto; the other two have 33 each), recounting the poet’s imagined journey through the afterlife, guided first by Virgil and then by Beatrice. It combines medieval theology, classical learning and contemporary politics.

## Why it matters

It helped establish Tuscan as a literary language and is regarded as a masterpiece of medieval European literature. It shaped later imagery of the afterlife and remains a major influence on poets and artists.
