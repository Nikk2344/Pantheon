A Mesopotamian poem about Gilgamesh, king of Uruk, his friendship with the wild man Enkidu, Enkidu’s death, and Gilgamesh’s failed search for immortality. The best-preserved form is the twelve-tablet Standard Babylonian version, assembled from earlier Sumerian and Old Babylonian material.

## Why it matters

It is among the oldest surviving works of narrative literature and shows how Mesopotamian writers treated friendship, mortality and kingship. Its flood episode is a well-studied parallel to other ancient flood narratives.
