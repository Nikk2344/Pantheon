## Why shape became a computational target

A protein's amino-acid sequence is related to the structures it can adopt. Knowing a structure can help researchers propose how it works or where other molecules might interact. Experimental methods remain essential, but obtaining structures for every target is difficult.

AlphaFold 2 brought a substantial improvement at CASP14, a community assessment using structures not available to predictors at the time of prediction. This matters because testing against concealed experimental targets is stronger evidence than choosing an attractive prediction after seeing the answer.

## Prediction and physical simulation are different

The model learns relationships between sequence information and structure. It does not simply simulate every atomic collision throughout the time a protein takes to fold. Calling the result a solution to every aspect of “protein folding” erases this distinction.

## Work through a research judgment

Imagine a predicted protein with a confident central region and a poorly determined tail. A researcher interested in the centre and one interested in the tail should not treat the prediction as equally informative. A visually complete rendering can hide uneven confidence; examine the uncertainty alongside the coordinates.

The experimental comparison linked below explains why predictions can accelerate structure determination while remaining hypotheses. Different conformations, weakly supported regions, and discrepancies with measurements require interpretation rather than automatic acceptance.

## What changes next?

The next problem is not merely drawing an isolated chain. Biological activity often involves interactions among molecules. [AlphaFold 3](/innovations/alphafold-3) broadens the molecular types considered, while retaining the need to distinguish a predicted arrangement from measured behaviour.
