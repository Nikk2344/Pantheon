## Learn an easier local task

Generating an entire image in one step is a difficult modelling problem. Diffusion breaks generation into successive transformations. During training, the noise level is known, so the model can learn corrections at many stages rather than receiving only a final aesthetic judgment.

The 2020 DDPM paper connected its formulation with denoising score matching and demonstrated strong image synthesis. Its historical predecessors matter: the method was an advance in an existing research programme, not the first idea of learning to reverse a noisy process.

## Imagine a cloud of dots

As a teaching analogy, imagine adding random displacement to dots that initially trace a circle. After enough corruption the circle becomes hard to see. A model trained across many such examples could learn local updates toward the family of circle-like configurations. This analogy explains a distributional task; it is not the actual neural image algorithm.

## Pixels, latents, and conditions

Latent diffusion performs denoising in a representation produced by an autoencoder, then decodes it into an image. This can reduce the cost of operating directly on every pixel. Conditioning can guide generation with text or other inputs, but guidance is not an assurance that every requested spatial or semantic relationship is satisfied.

## Evaluate more than appearance

Compare multiple unselected samples, test changes in the prompt, and distinguish visual appeal from faithful representation. For a historical reconstruction, a generated object should not be mistaken for archival evidence. For a diagram, verify labels and relationships separately.

[AlphaFold 3](/innovations/alphafold-3) uses diffusion in a different domain: predicting atomic arrangements. A shared family of techniques does not make those scientific outputs equivalent to ordinary pictures.
