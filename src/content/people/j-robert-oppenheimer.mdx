## More than one famous project

J. Robert Oppenheimer’s story connects theoretical physics, wartime institutions and public responsibility. The nickname “father of the atomic bomb” can obscure both his earlier research and the collective nature of the Manhattan Project.

His molecular work and his research on collapsing stars ask different questions: how can we simplify a complex quantum system, and what happens when gravity overwhelms the assumptions that keep a star stable?

## Knowledge and responsibility

After the war, Oppenheimer led the Institute for Advanced Study and remained involved in debates about science and public policy. His reading ranged across literature and philosophy, including the Bhagavad Gita. That interest should not be treated as proof that he represented Hindu philosophy as a whole.

The ethical question remains open for the reader: when knowledge makes a powerful technology possible, how should responsibility be shared among researchers, institutions and political leaders?
