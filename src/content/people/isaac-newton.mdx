Newton was born prematurely on Christmas Day 1642, small enough, his mother said, to fit
into a quart pot, and not expected to live. His father was already dead. When he was
three his mother remarried and left him with his grandmother, a separation he appears
never to have forgiven; a list of sins he compiled as a student includes "threatening my
father and mother Smith to burne them and the house over them."

## The plague years

In 1665 Cambridge closed because of plague and Newton went home to Woolsthorpe. He was
twenty-two. In roughly eighteen months there he laid down the beginnings of calculus,
worked out that white light is composite, and started thinking about whether the force
that holds objects on the Earth might extend as far as the Moon. He then largely put it
aside for twenty years.

<Aside>
**About the apple.** The story is not a later invention: Newton told it himself, and
William Stukeley recorded him telling it over tea in 1726. What Newton described was
watching an apple fall in the orchard and asking why it fell straight down toward the
Earth's centre — the beginning of the thought that gravity might reach much further. The
version where it strikes him on the head is embellishment added by others.
</Aside>

## What the Principia actually did

The insight people remember is "gravity". The achievement is narrower and much harder.
Kepler had established, from Tycho Brahe's observations, that planets move in ellipses,
sweep equal areas in equal times, and obey a fixed relation between orbital period and
distance. These were three separate empirical rules with no explanation. Newton showed
they are all consequences of one inverse-square force acting on a body obeying his laws
of motion — and that the same force, at the Earth's surface, is what makes things fall.

That is the unification: the apple and the Moon are the same problem. The simulation on
this page is the demonstration in miniature. There is exactly one force law in it, and
one knob. Circles, ellipses, parabolas and hyperbolas all fall out of it, along with
Kepler's equal areas, without anything being added.

Newton was reluctant to publish and did so largely because Edmond Halley pushed him,
edited him, and paid the printing costs personally.

## The rest of him

He spent more of his life on alchemy and on biblical chronology than on physics. Keynes,
who bought a trove of his unpublished papers at auction in 1936, concluded that Newton
was "not the first of the age of reason" but "the last of the magicians". Both halves
were the same project to him: reading a design he believed had been deliberately put
there.

He is buried in Westminster Abbey.
