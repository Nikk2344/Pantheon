Al-Kindi was a philosopher of Baghdad, often called the first of the Arabic philosophers. He was involved in the movement to translate Greek works into Arabic, and he wrote on metaphysics, optics, music and medicine.

## The central question

Can foreign philosophy be taken up faithfully within a new religious and cultural setting?

Many of his works are lost, and his dates are approximate. A treatise on cryptanalysis is credited to him, and its significance is judged by historians cautiously.
